Research

Black hole thermodynamics suggests that spacetime may emerge from microscopic degrees of freedom that are not yet known. Without knowing them, I can still study the nature of spacetime as in effective field theory: with general relativity and, where it breaks down, with modifications of gravity that, like general relativity, do not presuppose a fixed spacetime geometry. Black holes and cosmology are where such modifications matter most.

  • Black hole thermodynamics with membranesWith David KastorMembranes that change the couplings of gravity, and the membrane ergospheres in which they can carry negative energy.
  • Quantum black holes as fluctuating geometriesWith Martin BojowaldStatic quantum black holes and their superpositions, whose quantum corrections to Newton's potential are first order in the Planck length.
  • Quantum simulation of constrained systemsUsing expectation values and their fluctuations and correlations to predict and check what a quantum simulation measures.
  • Black holes and inflationWith Jennie Traschen and Sofia CorbàHow a black hole present from a preinflationary epoch may change the amount of inflation associated with a given potential.
  • Covariant cosmology and the bounceWith Martin Bojowald and Erick DuqueCovariance conditions for perturbed cosmologies, and what they say about the bounce of loop quantum cosmology.
  • A dark sector phase transitionA first order phase transition in a dark SU(2) sector, coupled to the Standard Model via the Higgs portal.
  • Supernova progenitorsWith J. Craig WheelerStellar evolution models of the red supergiant Betelgeuse, its rotation, and the waves from its interior. Stars like it end in core collapse, which leaves a compact object like a black hole or a neutron star.
