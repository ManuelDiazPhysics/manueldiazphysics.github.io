Black holes and inflation

How much a black hole present at the start of inflation changes the number of e-foldings.

With Jennie Traschen and Sofia Corbà. Paper in preparation.

A black hole before inflation

Inflation is usually studied in a homogeneous universe. If a black hole was left over from a preinflationary era, the spacetime during inflation is that of a black hole in de Sitter space. It has two horizons, the black hole horizon at radius rb and, farther out, the cosmological horizon at radius rc. The black hole can be no larger than the cosmological horizon, and the two horizons coincide for the largest possible black hole. The question is how the black hole changes the amount of inflation, counted in e-foldings, that a given potential produces.

A time that follows the inflaton

Without a black hole the inflaton is constant on slices of constant cosmological time. With a black hole it is not. Gregory, Kastor and Traschen found a time coordinate T, regular on both horizons, in which the slow roll solutions depend on T alone,

3γ dφ/dT = −dW/dφ ,

where W is the potential and γ = H when there is no black hole. Surfaces of constant T are surfaces of constant inflaton.

Counting e-foldings

Without a black hole, the length, area and volume of an inflating region all grow by the same factor, so any of them gives the same count. With a black hole they do not, and we count with volumes. The initial region is the region between the two horizons on a slice of constant T. Its outer edge moves out along an outgoing geodesic, and at the end of inflation the final region runs from the black hole horizon out to that geodesic. The number of e-foldings N follows from e3N = Vf/Vi, which gives the standard count when there is no black hole.

The next step is to complete the incorporation of the back reaction of the evolving inflaton on the metric.
